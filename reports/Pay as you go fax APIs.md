# Fund Notifyre once, fax for pennies

Yes, such a service exists. The best fit for a small US tax firm is **Notifyre's pay-as-you-go plan**: **$0.03 per page to send, no monthly fee, credits that never expire, no fax number needed to send, a REST API and email-to-fax, and a free HIPAA BAA with AES-256 encryption and ISO 27001** ([Notifyre](https://notifyre.com/us/online-fax)). The one catch is a **one-time $10 minimum top-up**. That is prepaid fax credit, not a fee. At 10 three-page faxes a month it lasts about 11 months, and a year of that volume costs roughly **$10.80 in total**. No reputable API-capable provider offers a literal $0 deposit. The hosted "pay at checkout" fax connectors that do (OhFax, SingleFax, PromptFax) cost 11 to 22 times more for a typical three-page fax, and they are months-old startups with no BAA on their pay-per-fax tiers, so they are a poor place to send client tax data. Telnyx is the only cheaper option per page ($0.007), but sending requires a **$1/month number**, which makes it more expensive than Notifyre at small-firm volumes. Notifyre has no official MCP server, so for Claude to send faxes the firm needs a small custom MCP wrapper (roughly a day's work) or Notifyre's email-to-fax. Before setting up any fax workflow, note that the **IRS now accepts Forms 2848 and 8821 online**, which removes the most common reason a tax firm faxes at all ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/tax-professionals/serve-your-clients)).

## Notifyre's $10 deposit beats every "no monthly fee" rival on true annual cost

Most of the cost here is in the fine print, so the table below counts every recurring charge the notes found, not just the advertised per-page rate. It covers US domestic sending only, for two volumes: one three-page fax a month and ten three-page faxes a month (360 pages a year).

The table shows that a "minimum deposit" and a "monthly fee" are different kinds of cost. Notifyre's $10 is money the firm spends on its own faxes over time. It only becomes a sunk cost if the firm stops faxing entirely, and even then the loss is under $10. Telnyx's $1 number charge is gone every month whether a single page goes out or not, and over a year it adds up to more than all of Notifyre's page charges at ten faxes a month. Telnyx catches up only at roughly 45 pages a month or more, which a small accounting firm will rarely reach.

The real hidden fee at Notifyre is on the **receiving** side. Send-only is free of recurring charges, but **receiving faxes requires a plan starting at $4.90/month** for one number and 200 incoming pages ([Notifyre](https://notifyre.com/us/online-fax)). If the firm needs agencies or clients to fax documents back, that $4.90 belongs in the budget. If inbound faxes can keep going to an existing line or e-fax account, the firm pays nothing extra.

## Telnyx advertises "no monthly fee" but cannot send without renting a number

Telnyx's fax pricing page says $0.007 per page with no monthly fee for fax usage. The fee shows up elsewhere: numbers cost **$1.00 per number per month** ([Telnyx pricing](https://telnyx.com/pricing/fax)), and the developer documentation lists "a Telnyx account, phone number, and Fax Application" as prerequisites, with the `from` field defined as the phone number the fax is sent from ([Telnyx docs](https://developers.telnyx.com/docs/programmable-fax/send-a-fax-api)). A July 2026 competitor blog says Telnyx can send without a number, but Telnyx's own docs say otherwise, and the official source should win. Telnyx's fax API page also adds "**+ SIP Trunking usage for transmission**" to the per-page rate, a charge the notes could not quantify ([Telnyx Fax API pricing](https://telnyx.com/pricing/fax-api)). On top of that, top-ups have a **$10 minimum**, and "Freemium" accounts cannot top up until they upgrade to a fully verified account ([Telnyx Billing](https://support.telnyx.com/en/articles/4280500-billing-setup-billing-groups)).

Telnyx does have one advantage: it is the only low-cost fax provider with an actively maintained official MCP server. `telnyx-mcp` (v7.24.0, published September 25, 2026) runs in "Code Mode." Instead of offering a `send_fax` tool, it gives the agent a sandbox where it writes code against the full Telnyx SDK ([GitHub](https://github.com/team-telnyx/telnyx-node/tree/master/packages/mcp-server); [npm](https://registry.npmjs.org/telnyx-mcp)). That SDK includes fax resources and delivery webhooks ([telnyx-node api.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/team-telnyx/telnyx-node/master/api.md)), so Claude could send a fax this way. But the same API key also lets Claude buy numbers, place calls, and change account settings. For a firm that wants an agent to send a fax and nothing else, that is far more power than the job needs.

The other developer APIs leave important questions open. **Sinch** (which absorbed Phaxio and now receives ClickSend's former fax customers) lists $0.045 per page officially, while a third party says $0.07 plus a $2/month number. Its full rate card and minimum deposit could not be checked ([Sinch](https://sinch.com/voice/fax-api/); [ClickSend](https://www.clicksend.com/us/pricing/us/)). **SignalWire** bills $0.0095 per minute with a $5 entry top-up, but whether it needs a rented number was not verified ([SignalWire](https://signalwire.com/pricing/fax)). Several familiar names are ruled out: **Twilio shut off fax on December 17, 2021** ([Twilio](https://support.twilio.com/hc/en-us/articles/223136667-Fax-Support-on-Twilio)), ClickSend no longer offers fax to new customers, Vonage's API does not support fax ([Vonage](https://api.support.vonage.com/hc/en-us/articles/360038592671-Can-I-send-fax-over-the-Vonage-Voice-API)), and Faxage ($3.49/month plus $5 setup) and Documo ($25/month) both charge subscriptions ([Faxage](https://www.faxage.com/pricing.php); [Documo](https://www.documo.com/pricing)).

## Ready-made fax MCP servers trade price and safety for zero setup

If the requirement is "Claude can fax today, with no deposit," the official MCP Registry now lists several hosted, pay-per-fax connectors that meet it exactly. All of them were registered between May and September 2026 ([MCP Registry](https://registry.modelcontextprotocol.io/v0/servers?search=fax&limit=100)). **SingleFax** charges $0.99 per fax for up to 10 pages to the US, Canada, and Puerto Rico, and bills only when the fax is delivered ([SingleFax](https://singlefax.com)). **OhFax** charges $0.99 plus $0.25 per page, with "no subscription, no monthly minimum," a full refund if the fax fails, and deletion of documents within 24 hours ([OhFax](https://www.ohfax.com)). **PromptFax** charges $2.00 for up to five pages, needs no account, and shows a Stripe checkout before every send. That checkout doubles as a human approval step ([PromptFax](https://promptfax.app)). Prepaid-credit services such as Edwyna ($4 per fax, built on FaxZero) and NoErrands ($1.49 for the first page) allow fully autonomous sending, but their minimum top-ups were not published ([Edwyna](https://edwyna.com); [NoErrands](https://noerrands.com)).

At a few faxes a year, convenience is cheap enough not to matter. At ten faxes a month, SingleFax costs about eleven times as much as Notifyre, and PromptFax about twenty-two times. The bigger problem is trust. These are small, new vendors with no independent security reviews found. Only SingleFax mentions a BAA, and only on its paid team plans. Every client document would pass through a company the firm has no contract with.

Among subscription providers, **Fax.Plus** has the most complete official fax MCP (send, receive, track, contacts) at `mcp.fax.plus`. However, it excludes the Free plan and, notably, **excludes accounts with HIPAA Advanced Security Controls** turned on ([Fax.Plus MCP](https://www.fax.plus/mcp)). In other words, its MCP cannot be used on the security setting a firm handling sensitive data would choose. Zapier and Pipedream expose fax actions for Fax.Plus, Documo, iFax, Sinch/Phaxio, and Telnyx, but they add a second bill and a second data processor without lowering the fax price, and Zapier has no Notifyre MCP ([Zapier](https://zapier.com/mcp/faxplus); [Pipedream](https://pipedream.com/apps/phaxio/actions/send-fax)).

## A one-day custom MCP turns Notifyre into a safe agent fax tool

Since Notifyre has no MCP, the practical path is a small wrapper the firm controls. A fax MCP needs only three tools: get a quote, send a fax, and check its status. That is the same small set the hosted connectors use. SendThisFax, for example, exposes `get_price`, `send_fax`, `get_fax_status`, and `check_balance` ([SendThisFax](https://www.sendthisfax.com/en/mcp)). The open-source **faxdrop-mcp** project (MIT license, actively released) shows the safety controls worth copying, all in a small codebase: a dry-run flag, a recipient allowlist with "pairing" mode, phone-number format validation, a 10 MB file cap, a folder restriction so the agent can only send files from one outbox directory, and a JSONL audit log with restricted file permissions that redacts sensitive fields ([GitHub faxdrop-mcp](https://github.com/klodr/faxdrop-mcp)). The research team estimates 100 to 200 lines of code and about half a day to a day to build a version with these controls, running locally for Claude Code or Claude Desktop. If the firm wants it as a claude.ai connector, deploying it as an authenticated hosted server adds about another day. If the firm would rather not build anything, **Faxbot** is an existing self-hosted fax gateway that ships an MCP server and supports Sinch/Phaxio and SignalWire, though not Notifyre ([GitHub Faxbot](https://github.com/DMontgomery40/Faxbot)).

For a tax firm, the wrapper's design is a compliance choice as much as an engineering one. Faxes cannot be recalled, so the send tool should never be auto-approved in Claude Code. It should require a confirmation token returned by a prior quote call, check the destination against a list of approved agency and bank numbers, cap pages and daily spend, and log the number, page count, a file hash, and the provider's fax ID without logging the document's contents. Notifyre's API and email-to-fax are advertised alongside its PAYG pricing, but the notes do not confirm that API access is included on the PAYG tier or what its exact endpoint parameters are. Spend five minutes confirming both with Notifyre before putting money in.

## Client tax data rules out the free and novelty options

The IRS says tax preparers must keep a written information security plan, a requirement that comes from the FTC Safeguards Rule ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/heres-what-tax-preparers-need-to-know-about-a-data-security-plan)). IRS Publication 4557 sets the baseline safeguards around it ([Intuit on Pub 4557](https://accountants.intuit.com/taxprocenter/practice-management/how-to-update-your-tax-firms-data-safeguards-based-on-irs-pub-4557/)). Neither appears to contain fax-specific rules. Their general principles still decide the question: the firm should use vendors that encrypt data and that it can oversee by contract. Free web-form services fail that test. FaxZero's free tier caps faxes at three pages, adds its branding to the cover sheet, and its privacy page returned a 404 error. GotFreeFax leaves retention terms to policies the researchers could not read ([FaxZero](https://faxzero.com/); [GotFreeFax](https://www.gotfreefax.com/)). Notifyre (free BAA, ISO 27001, AES-256 in transit and at rest) and Sinch (HIPAA with a BAA) provide the kind of documentation a security plan can cite ([Notifyre](https://notifyre.com/us/online-fax); [Sinch](https://sinch.com/voice/fax-api/)). Telnyx states HIPAA support on its pricing page ([Telnyx](https://telnyx.com/pricing/fax-api)).

The cheapest fax is the one the firm never sends. The IRS accepts Forms 2848 and 8821 through **Tax Pro Account**, where the client signs digitally, and through **Submit Forms 2848 and 8821 Online**, which requires a Secure Access account. Faxed or mailed copies still need handwritten signatures ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/tax-professionals/serve-your-clients); [Thomson Reuters](https://tax.thomsonreuters.com/news/irs-rolls-out-online-power-of-attorney-and-tax-information-authorization-forms/)). Moving authorizations to these online channels leaves fax for the smaller set of state agencies, some IRS units, and banks that still require it, which is the low-volume use case where a pay-per-page plan works best.

## Conclusion

The question assumed that the best deal would be the provider with no minimum. The numbers point the other way. For an occasional sender, the monthly line items (number rental, receive plans, platform fees) do the real damage, while a small prepaid deposit that never expires costs nothing extra. Notifyre's $10 is less than one year of Telnyx's number rental and about the price of ten SingleFax sends. The hosted MCPs that do meet "no minimum" literally are useful for a one-off non-sensitive fax, but they are the wrong default for documents containing Social Security numbers.

The agent piece has also become easy. In late 2026 the hard part of letting Claude fax is no longer the plumbing. Either it is a single npm package (Telnyx) or a one-day wrapper (Notifyre). The real work is limiting what the agent is allowed to do. A narrow fax-only tool with an allowlist and a confirm-before-send step, sitting on a provider with a signed BAA, is safer than any general-purpose telecom MCP, and it is the setup a firm can describe in its written security plan. The remaining unknowns are worth a quick email to the vendors before committing: whether Notifyre's API is included on PAYG, Sinch's actual rate and deposit, and Telnyx's SIP transmission charge.
